# Setting & Tone — STATUS: PLAN

## Era and place

**Tanah Melayu, c. 1400s — Kesultanan Melaka at its peak**, the Hang Tuah era.

Melaka at this point is the richest entrepôt in the Straits: a port city where
Gujarati, Chinese, Javanese, Arab and Tamil traders unload at the same wharf,
governed by the *Undang-Undang Melaka* and a Bendahara's court. Wealth,
hierarchy, and a great deal that the court would rather not look at directly.

The brief places the early game at the **edges** of that prosperity, not its
centre:

- **Pinggir Muara Hangus** — the burnt estuary fringe
- **Pangkalan Pasar Gelap** — the black-market wharf
- **Paya Keramat Lama** — the old sacred swamp

That is a deliberate and good choice. The player starts where Melaka's golden
age does not reach.

## Aesthetic

**Dark Fantasy Melayu / Silat Gritty.** The brief cites *Nirnama* as the
reference point.

What that implies concretely:

- Muted, humid palette — mud, brass, tarnished silver, dried blood, lamp-oil
  orange. Not the saturated greens the game currently uses.
- Violence with weight and consequence. Weapons are *keris*, *parang*,
  *kerambit* — short, intimate, personal. Not heroic broadswords.
- The supernatural is **folk-real**, not spectacle: things people actually
  feared, in the places they feared them.

## Social strata

Three tiers, and the brief is right that this should drive more than flavour:

| Tier | Who | Narrative role |
|---|---|---|
| **Orang Kampung** | villagers, fishers, traders | the ones who suffer; quest-givers, victims, informants |
| **Orang Istana** (Bangsawan) | court nobility | patrons and threats; wield law and favour, rarely violence directly |
| **Hulubalang** | warriors sworn to a lord | the player's likely social position, or the thing they are cast out of |

**This is a systems opportunity, not just backstory.** A standing question in
[open-questions.md](open-questions.md): should social tier be a *mechanic* —
who talks to you, which districts admit you, whose protection you carry — or
purely narrative colour? The brief does not say, and it changes a lot.

## The supernatural: jin, saka, santau

The brief's bestiary splits enemies cleanly into **Manusia** (human criminals)
and **Jin** (folk-supernatural entities): toyol, pelesit, saka, hantu jerut,
jin tanah.

This split is the strongest structural idea in the document. It gives:

- Two enemy tracks with genuinely different counterplay
- A tonal contrast — human cruelty vs. inhuman hunger
- A natural axis for gear and abilities (steel vs. *penawar* / warding)

**It also needs a deliberate decision about how far into real belief to go.**
These are not invented monsters. Toyol, saka, santau and pelesit are live folk
belief in Malaysia and the wider region, entangled with Islamic teaching about
the unseen. The brief already shows awareness of this — the Pawang's line
*"Bukan hamba pada iblis, cuma mengutip janji yang ditabur tanah"* is doing
careful work, positioning the class as transactional with the land rather than
in service to the devil.

That instinct is worth making an explicit rule rather than leaving to
individual writing sessions. See [open-questions.md](open-questions.md) Q9 —
it affects the Pawang class most, and it affects whether the game is
comfortable to release to a Malaysian audience.

## Story frame — **not yet specified**

The brief covers world, classes and enemies. It does **not** yet answer:

- **Who is the player?** A disgraced hulubalang? A villager who lost someone?
  An outsider? This determines the opening and the social-tier question above.
- **What is the inciting incident?** Why is this person at the burnt estuary
  killing bandits and jin?
- **What is the Lv20 boss pair *for*?** Tok Begal and the Saka are framed as
  "end of early game" — but end of what arc?
- **Is there an antagonist above them?** The two-track split (human/jin)
  suggests a convergence later. Nothing states it.

A proposal, offered to be argued with rather than accepted:

> The player is a **hulubalang without a lord** — sworn service ended badly, by
> death or disgrace. Melaka's prosperity has no place for a warrior with no
> master, so they work the estuary edge. The human track is what desperate
> people do to each other when the court stops looking. The jin track is what
> moves in when a place stops being tended. **Both are symptoms of the same
> neglect**, which is what makes them one story instead of two.

That frame makes the social tiers load-bearing, explains why the player fights
both tracks, and gives the Lv20 bosses a shared meaning: one is a man who
became a monster, the other a monster that was once a man's weapon.

## What this replaces

Everything currently in-game. The existing setting is unnamed generic fantasy —
slimes in a green field. There is no story, no faction, no place-name. Nothing
to preserve, which makes this cheap narratively and expensive artistically.
