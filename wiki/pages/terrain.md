# Terrain & Map Generation

Added 2026-09-06, replacing the single stretched grass quad that
[`world.tscn`](scenes.md) used to draw the whole arena with.

## Three pieces

| Piece | Where |
|---|---|
| Atlas image | `assets/sprites/tileset_terrain.png` — generated by `create_terrain_atlas()` in [`generate_sprites.py`](tools.md) |
| TileSet | `assets/tilesets/terrain.tres` — 37 declared tiles, one atlas source |
| Painting | `_build_map()` and friends in [`world.gd`](world.md) |

## Atlas layout

384×160 at a 32 px grid, matching the existing sprite scale.

| Region | Cells |
|---|---|
| Grass variants (4) | `(0..3, 0)` |
| Dirt nine-slice | `(0..2, 1..3)` |
| Water nine-slice | `(4..6, 1..3)` |
| Stone nine-slice | `(8..10, 1..3)` |
| Props | `(0..5, 4)` — rock, bush, flowers, stump, tall grass, cliff |

Each 3×3 block is a plain nine-slice: `NW N NE / W C E / SW S SE`.

**Edge tiles blend into grass**, they are not just a darkened fill. `_blit_block`
draws a grass background, then lays the terrain over it with a jittered
per-scanline inset so the boundary is ragged rather than a straight line. That
is what makes a dirt patch read as part of the world instead of a coloured
rectangle — the first version darkened the edges instead and looked exactly
like coloured rectangles.

The generator is seeded (`20260906`), so regenerating the atlas is
reproducible.

## Why not Godot terrain sets

Godot 4 has a built-in Terrains/autotile system driven by per-tile peering
bits. This project does the neighbour matching itself in `world.gd`
(`_slice_offset`) instead, for two reasons:

- Peering bits are configured in the TileSet editor. Hand-authoring them in a
  `.tres` is verbose and very easy to get subtly wrong with no way to check.
- Doing it in GDScript makes the logic **testable** — see
  `_test_procedural_map` in [run-game-tests.md](run-game-tests.md), which
  asserts that a water region has both interior and edge tiles.

Godot 4's terrain matching is also documented as less reliable than Godot 3's
autotiles, so the built-in path was not obviously the safer one.

## Map layout

`_lay_out_terrain()` decides a terrain kind per cell **before** anything is
painted, so the layout can be reasoned about independently of tiles:

- Grass fill across ±38 tiles (±1216 px — slightly wider than the ±1200 arena
  walls, so a player can never see the map edge)
- Wobbling dirt crossroads through the middle, offset by a sine/cosine so they
  do not read as ruled lines
- A water pond to the south-east (two overlapping blobs)
- A stone clearing centred on the boss spawn at world `(0, -450)`
- Two smaller dirt clearings

`_stamp_blob()` draws a circle with a jittered radius, giving organic outlines.

`_scatter_props()` then places decoration on the **Detail** layer, only on open
grass — never on a path or in water.

## Determinism matters here

Every client generates its own map; nothing about the terrain is synchronised.
That is safe **only** because generation is deterministic:

- A fixed `MAP_SEED` drives the `RandomNumberGenerator`.
- Grass variants come from a positional hash, not the RNG, so the result does
  not depend on dictionary iteration order.

`_test_procedural_map` instantiates the world **twice** and asserts both the
terrain layout and the painted atlas coordinates match. If that ever fails,
clients are seeing different maps.

## Layers and depth

`Map/Ground` sits at `z_index = -100` and `Map/Detail` at `-50`, both beneath
the Y-sorted entities ([scenes.md](scenes.md)).

That means props are flat decoration — a bush does **not** occlude a player
walking behind it. Making props occlude would mean moving them out of the
TileMapLayer into Y-sorted nodes, or splitting them onto a layer above the
entities with per-cell Y-sort. Not done; noted here so the choice is visible.

## Wading

Water does not block movement — it **slows** it, and deeper water slows more.

`_compute_water_depth()` runs a breadth-first search outward from every dry
cell, so depth 1 is the shoreline and it grows toward the middle of a pond. The
map is precomputed once, so `speed_multiplier_at_world()` is a dictionary
lookup — cheap enough to call per frame.

```
multiplier = clamp(1.0 - depth * WATER_SLOW_PER_DEPTH, WATER_SLOW_FLOOR, 1.0)
```

With the current pond that lands around **0.84x at the shoreline and 0.52x in
the middle**, floored at 0.35.

Both [`player.gd`](player.md) and [`monster.gd`](monster.md) apply it —
monsters wade too, so a pond is a real obstacle rather than a shortcut only the
player has to respect. The dodge roll is slowed as well.

## Cliffs

`Terrain.CLIFF` regions are **impassable high ground**, stamped into the four
far corners.

Two constraints shape where they can go:

- Well outside the monster spawn ring (every spawn point sits within ~14 tiles
  of the origin), so nothing spawns inside a cliff.
- Any cliff cell landing on the crossroads is cleared back to grass
  (`_is_on_road`). Without that, a badly placed blob could cut the arena in
  half.

Collision is built by `_build_cliff_collision()`. Rather than one body per cell
— currently 610 of them — adjacent cells are merged into horizontal runs and
each run becomes a single rectangle: **86 shapes instead of 610**.

Note `TileMapLayer` cells are top-left anchored while collision shapes are
centred, which `_add_cliff_run()` corrects for.

## Known gaps

- The four pillars are still hand-placed `StaticBody2D` nodes with their own
  sprites rather than tiles.
- Cliff tiles have no edge blending — a cliff region has a hard rectangular
  boundary against the grass, unlike the dirt/water/stone nine-slices.
